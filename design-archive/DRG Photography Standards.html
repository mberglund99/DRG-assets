DRG Design System  ·  Section 07

Photography

DRG sells high-end coastal homes, and the photography has to look like it before anyone walks through the door. This section is a reference for what good looks like — the frames worth getting, the light worth waiting for, and how to use the files once they land. The examples throughout are from 1120 Harborway Place.

1120 Harborway Place · golden hour · the frame to aim for
01What Good Looks Like

Photograph the light, not the room

Three qualities run through every DRG image worth using.

Warm

Wood, linen, low sun, water. Warm surfaces stay warm and walls stay white. Never the flat blue-gray of a rental listing.

Wide, not distorted

Wide enough to read the space, never so wide the walls bow. When verticals lean, the house looks cheaper than it is.

Lived-in, no people

A tray on the island, a boat at the dock, cushions that have been sat in. Homes only — no people, no pets, no visible brands.

02The Frames

Four frames worth having

Each one does a different job. A gallery that skips one leaves money on the table.

A · Exterior Hero

Show the house

Square to the facade or three-quarter, camera around six feet, sun off one shoulder. This is the frame that ends up on signage, postcards and the MLS lead image, so it deserves the most patience.

B · Golden Hour

Show the life

The reason someone pays the premium. Shot in the last forty minutes of light with the interior lights on, so the house reads warm against the sky. One shoot, one chance — stage before the sun drops.

C · Interior Light

Show the space

Shot toward the windows rather than away from them, so the view stays in the frame instead of blowing out. Lights on, fans off, blinds at one consistent angle.

D · Aerial — waterfront

Show the water

Reserved for waterfront. A high oblique proves a lake, marsh or oceanfront claim in a single frame — and shows the land the house sits on. Mid-morning, when the water reads clearest.

03Two Packages

Not every listing needs the same shoot

Two tiers keep quality consistent without overspending on a condo or underspending on a waterfront estate.

Tier 01

Signature

  • Exterior hero, front and rear
  • Full interior walk, every principal room
  • One golden-hour frame
  • 20–25 edited stills
  • Aerial if the home touches water
Typical for listings under the local luxury threshold
Tier 02

Premier

  • Everything in Signature
  • Dedicated golden-hour session, exterior and outdoor living
  • Aerial set — two top-down, four oblique
  • Twilight exterior with interior lights on
  • 35+ edited stills
  • Walkthrough video
Luxury and waterfront listings
On choosing a photographer: agents can book whoever they trust, but send them this page first. The approved vendor list exists because those shooters already work this way — using it is the shortest path to a gallery that matches the rest of the brokerage.
04Light & Timing

Book the shoot around the sun

ExteriorsFront-facing homes in the morning, west-facing in the afternoon. Worth checking which way the facade points before the shoot is booked.
InteriorsMid-morning to early afternoon, when there is enough daylight to shoot into the windows.
Golden hourRoughly forty minutes before sunset through ten minutes after. Arrive an hour early and stage first.
Aerials9:00–11:00am. High sun, short shadows, and water at its clearest green.
Gray daysReschedule if the forecast is flat overcast. A launch delayed by two days beats a gray hero image on the sign.
05Using the Files

Use the crops you were sent

Every hero frame comes back in four ratios. Cropping a wide image down to a square on the way into a post is where good photography goes wrong.

3 : 2
Print, MLS, letter and postcard. The master crop.
4 : 5
Instagram and Facebook feed. Vertical room to breathe.
16 : 9
Slides and web hero. Lower third left clear for type.
1 : 1
Listing grids and the six-up sold sheet. House centered.
File naming331-oceanaire-ln_hero_3x2.jpg — address, shot type, ratio. Makes the right file findable a year later.
FormatJPEG, sRGB. Full-resolution masters archived; web copies capped at 2400px on the long edge.
RightsConfirm in writing that DRG can use the images across print, web and social for as long as it needs them.
06Editing

Where it goes wrong

Looks right
  • Verticals parallel to the frame edge.
  • Natural white balance; warm floors stay warm.
  • Sky keeps its cloud detail and gradient.
  • Grass and water in their real colors.
  • Flare only when the sun is deliberately in frame.
Looks wrong
  • Furniture out of place — a dropped umbrella, a stray cushion, a hose in frame.
  • HDR halos, crushed blacks, plastic skies.
  • Oversaturated grass or turquoise-pushed water.
  • Replaced skies, heavy vignettes, film grain.
  • Watermarks across the image — credit belongs in the caption.
One grade per listing. A gallery should look like one afternoon by one photographer. Mixed grades inside a single gallery are the fastest way to look like mixed quality across the brokerage.
07Type on Photography

How type sits on an image

Photographs carry type with a scrim behind it, or with type given its own field.

Just Sold
1120 Harborway Place
Bottom scrim · default

Slate-teal gradient from 78% at the base to clear at two-thirds height. Dune label, linen headline, both at full opacity.

Now Showing
1120 Harborway Place
Split panel · for longer copy

When the caption runs past a few words, give type a solid field instead of fighting the photograph. Panel takes about 40% of the frame.

If the headline is hard to read, move the headline. Darkening the whole photograph to rescue a caption costs more than it saves.
08Agent Headshots

One look across the roster

Headshots appear on letters, signage, decks and the site. Consistency between agents matters more than any single portrait.

Circular crop · correspondence

Used at small sizes in letters, email signatures and deck footers. Eyes on the upper third, shoulders squared, generous margin so the crop never clips hair.

4 : 5 crop · signage and web

Shot outdoors in open shade or indoors against a warm neutral. Business attire, no strong patterns. Natural expression — a real smile beats a corporate one.

Retake every two to three years, and whenever an agent's appearance changes noticeably. A dated headshot undercuts the credibility everything else is working to build.
09Video

Video, briefly

Where it belongs

Premier listings. A walkthrough that moves the way a buyer would, not a drone reel set to music.

Same light rules

Shot in the same window as the stills, on the same day, so the video and the gallery look like the same house.

Delivery

One long cut for the listing page, one vertical cut under sixty seconds for social. Both branded with the DRG mark at the close.
