The Opening
Move 01 · Connect03

Don't start with the presentation

Start with the person standing in front of you. Your first objective is not to explain DRG, prove your production, or open the packet. It is to understand what this house has been and what the next chapter is supposed to look like.

The home is the conversation starter. Let them give you the tour they are proud of, and take notes while they do it. Everything you need to price, market and close is in what they tell you in the first twenty minutes.

Leave the packet in the car until it's earned. Pulling out materials early turns a conversation into a pitch, and a pitch invites comparison shopping.
Do this
Arrive earlyDrive the street. Note what is for sale, what just sold, what the neighbors keep up.
Compliment honestlyOne specific thing, not four general ones. "That dock is in better shape than most on this water."
Take notes visiblyWriting down their answers tells them the answers matter. It also gives you their words to quote back later.
Hold your numbersIf they ask for a price in the first ten minutes: "I'll give you a real answer before I leave. Show me the house first."
Watch the roomPacked boxes, a second set of keys, a spouse who stays quiet. Motivation shows before it is spoken.

The Opening
Move 01 · Connect04

Questions that create conversation

Natural questions, real answers, no interrogation. Open with the same line every time — "Tell me about your home" — and follow wherever it goes.

About the home
"What's your favorite part of living here?"
  • Where do you drink coffee in the morning?
  • Which room do you actually spend time in?
  • What have you put into it since you bought?
  • What would you fix if you were staying?
Their favorite room is your hero photograph. What they fixed is your value story.
About the move
"What are you going to miss?"
  • Where are you thinking about going?
  • Where did y'all move here from?
  • What's driving the timing?
  • Have you already found something?
The answer to timing decides pricing strategy more than any comp will.
About the decision
"Have you talked to anyone else about selling?"
  • What would make this feel like a win?
  • What worried you last time you sold?
  • Who else is part of this decision?
  • What do you need from me to feel good about it?
Asking this directly is not rude. It is the fastest way to find the real objection.
If a question can be answered with yes or no, rework it. And when they answer, count to two before you speak — the second sentence is usually the honest one.

The Walk-Through
Move 02 · Discover05

Walk the home. Connect the dots.

Every room is two questions: what is it worth, and what is it worth to them. Ask the second one out loud and the first one gets easier.

PoolDo you use it a lot? · Heater, liner, pump age. Buyers ask; know the answer.
Dock & waterAre y'all boaters? · Depth at low tide, lift capacity, permits. This is the number one waterfront question.
KitchenIs this where everyone gathers? · Appliance age, renovation year, what they'd change.
Yard & outdoorDo you spend much time out here? · Irrigation, drainage, what the light does in the evening.
SystemsWhat's been replaced since you bought? · Roof, HVAC, water heater, windows. Documented updates defend price.
NeighborhoodHow have you liked the neighbors? · HOA, dues, amenities, who else is thinking of selling.
Next moveWhat are you looking for next? · A second transaction usually sits on the other side of this one.
Write down their phrases. "We eat every dinner on that porch" is better listing copy than anything you'll write at your desk.
Flag the problems out loud, gently. Naming the dated bath yourself builds more trust than pretending not to see it — and sets up the pricing conversation.

Discovery
Move 02 · Discover06

Listen for the four big things

You cannot price a house correctly without knowing why it is being sold. These four answers set the strategy; everything after this page is built on them.

01

Why

Why are they selling?
Job, family, downsizing, divorce, an estate. The reason tells you how much of the decision is financial and how much is not.
02

Where

Where are they going?
Already under contract somewhere else is a different appointment than "we're just thinking about it."
03

When

When do they need to move?
A date makes the pricing conversation simple. No date means you are selling the plan, not the price.
04

What

What matters most?
Top dollar, speed, privacy, or as little disruption as possible. Ask them to rank it. Most sellers never have been.
Say it back before you leave. "So — Charleston by June, you'd rather net well than move fast, and you don't want signs in the yard. Did I get that right?" Nothing builds confidence faster than being accurately understood.

Positioning DRG
Move 03 · Position08

Say it in ninety seconds

The DRG story is short on purpose. Sellers do not remember a capabilities deck; they remember one clear reason you are the right call. Lead with the database and the builder relationships — then use their own answers to pick what comes next.

The ninety seconds

"We're not a general brokerage. We work inside these communities alongside the developers and builders every day, so we see pricing and product before it hits the MLS — and we have a database of more than a hundred and fifty thousand buyers planning a move to this coast. Your house goes in front of them the week it's ready. Then it's me the whole way, not a handoff. That's the difference."

Then stop. The instinct after a good ninety seconds is to add a fifth point. Ask a question instead: "Which part of that matters most to you?"
Lead with this, based on what you heard
"We need to move fast"The database. Your buyer may already be on our list — that is the shortest path to an offer.
"We want top dollar"Audience plus presentation. More of the right buyers in the first ten days is what moves the top of the range.
"We're comparing to new construction"Builder access. We know what's releasing, at what price, and how your home stacks up against it.
"Our last sale was rough"One agent, start to finish. Name what went wrong last time and how this is different.
"We're interviewing a few"All four, in order, then ask what the others proposed. You want to be last, not first.
"We know a guy"Don't compete on relationship. Compete on the plan, and leave the plan in writing.
Never sell against another agent by name. Describe what we do and let the comparison make itself.

Choose the Route
Decision point09

Two routes. One objective.

Read the room after the walk-through, then commit. The mistake is trying to close a seller who isn't ready, or leaving with homework when they were ready to sign.

Route 01

Ready to go

The relationship is there, the answers were open, and pricing is the only thing left. Don't overcomplicate it — move into the three prices and ask for the business today.
Signals
  • They gave you a real timeline and a destination
  • Both decision-makers are in the room and agreeing
  • They ask what you would list it for
  • They start talking about what to fix before photos
Route 02

CMA callback

Something is unresolved — the number, the spouse, the timing, or you. Don't force it. Earn a second meeting, which you are more likely to win than a rushed first one.
Signals
  • "We're still just exploring"
  • One spouse is quiet or disagrees
  • They name a price well outside the comps
  • They're interviewing other agents
  • You don't yet know why they're selling
Either way, you leave with a date. Route 01 ends with a signed information sheet and a shoot on the calendar. Route 02 ends with a specific day and time to come back with comps. "I'll follow up next week" is not a route.

Pricing Strategy
Move 04 · Price10

Three prices. Three purposes.

Give the seller a strategic choice, not a single number. One number invites an argument; three numbers turn the conversation into a decision about what they want.

Top spot

Test the market

The highest price the comps can defend. Right when the seller has real time and no pressure.
Expect
  • Fewer showings, slower start
  • A price adjustment inside 30–45 days
  • Buyers who compare you to better-finished homes
Sweet spot

Position the home

Where the comps and the condition actually put it. The recommendation nine times out of ten.
Expect
  • Steady traffic in the first ten days
  • Offers near ask, room to negotiate
  • The strongest balance of price and probability
Sell spot

Create activity

Deliberately aggressive, built for speed. For a hard deadline, a second mortgage, or an estate.
Expect
  • Heavy early traffic, possibly multiple offers
  • A faster close, a lower net
  • A decision needed in days, not weeks
Show the range, recommend one. "All three are defensible. Based on your June date, I'd list at the sweet spot." Sellers want a choice and an opinion, in that order.
Price the market, not the seller's hopes. If they push past top spot, agree to revisit in two weeks in writing — with the showings and feedback in hand, the market makes the argument for you.

Commission
Move 04 · Price11

Bring up commission first

Never make a seller drag the number out of you. The agent who raises commission early sounds like it is worth what it costs; the agent who waits to be asked sounds like they were hoping it wouldn't come up.

Say the number plainly, then say what it buys — and what happens to the buy-side fee — before they have to ask a second question. Two sentences, no apology, no filler.

Say it like this

"Before we go further, let's talk about what this costs. Our commission is ___, and here's exactly what it covers. On the buyer's side, ___ — and that's negotiable in the offer, so I'll walk you through it when we get there."

What it buys — name these out loud
PresentationFull photography package, video, aerials when the property touches water, and every crop the marketing needs. Not billed separately.
ReachOur 150,000-buyer relocation database first, then MLS and syndication, social, print, and a mailing to the neighborhood that already wants to know the number.
Pricing workThe comps, the strategy, and the discipline to adjust with data instead of panic.
NegotiationOffer to close: inspection, repairs, appraisal, financing. This is where the fee earns itself back or doesn't.
Your timeShowings, calls, vendors, deadlines. One point of contact who answers.
Put it on paper. A one-page list of what the fee covers, handed over as you say the number, ends most of the conversation before it starts.

Commission
Holding the fee12

When they push on the fee

Answer calmly, once, and move on. Discounting the moment you are asked tells the seller the first number was soft — and invites the same negotiation on every decision after it.

"Another agent quoted less"

"They might do it for less. The question is what comes off the plan to get there — usually the photography and the marketing, which is the part that moves your price."

"Will you cut your side?"

"I don't discount the work, because I'd have to do less of it. What I can do is show you what each piece costs if we ran it separately — it comes out higher."

"It's a lot on a house this price"

"It is real money. It's also the difference between a number on a sign and a closing at the top of the range. One percent on presentation usually returns more than one percent."

"Can we do less on the buyer side?"

"We can — and it's your call. Here's the tradeoff: buyer agents sort by what's offered, and the homes offering less get shown less. Let's decide it on purpose, not by accident."

"What if you sell it yourself?"

"Then the fee is structured differently, and I'll show you exactly how before you sign anything. That's in the agreement, not a handshake."

"Let us think about it"

"Of course. What part would you like to think about — the price, the fee, or the timing? Whichever it is, I'd rather work through it now than leave you guessing."

One answer, then silence. Repeating yourself or stacking three justifications reads as doubt. Say it once, hold it, and let the plan do the rest of the work.

The Launch
Move 04 · Price13

Sell the launch, not just the listing

A seller should leave the appointment thinking: these people have a plan. Give the plan dates — photography, video and marketing can be ready in about a week.

Days 1–2

Strategy

Paperwork, final price, disclosures, and a punch list of what to fix or clear before the camera shows up.
Days 3–5

Prep

Shoot booked around the light. Stills, video, aerial if it touches water. Staged before the sun drops, not during.
Days 6–7

Build

MLS copy, print, social, sign and the neighborhood mailing — plus the database email, which goes out before the listing is public.
Day 8

Launch

Live on a Thursday, first showings that weekend, feedback to the seller every Monday from there.
Then ask the question that ends the appointment: "When would you want to go live?" A date turns the plan into a commitment, and gives you the reason to call tomorrow.

Commitment
Move 05 · Ask14

Turn the conversation into a transaction

Once they say yes, make the next step small. Don't leave to "send some paperwork over" — fill out the information sheet at the kitchen table while the decision is fresh and both of them are sitting there.

Ask the questions out loud and write the answers down. It takes fifteen minutes, it saves a week of email, and it makes the listing feel underway rather than pending.

Before you stand up: a signed agreement or a shoot date, the punch list of what they'll handle before photos, and how they want to hear from you. Then confirm all three in an email the same night.
The information sheet
Property
  • Legal owners and contact details
  • Year built, updates and dates
  • Square footage and lot
  • Systems: roof, HVAC, water heater
  • HOA, dues, amenities
  • Special features and exclusions
Terms
  • Preferred list date
  • Occupancy and showing instructions
  • Pets, alarm codes, gate access
  • Desired closing timeframe
  • Communication preference and cadence
  • Who else must sign
The exclusions question is the one agents skip and regret. Ask it plainly: "Is there anything in the house that isn't staying — a chandelier, the swing, the beer fridge?"

The second meeting15

Come back with a plan

When you're unsure, don't force the close — earn the second conversation. Most listings that feel lost at the kitchen table are won two days later by the agent who came back prepared.

Before you leave the first meeting
Name the gap"Sounds like the number is the open question. Let me put the comps together and show you three ways to price it."
Set the dateA day and a time, in their calendar, before you walk out. Thursday at six, not "later this week."
Get the roomAsk for both decision-makers and twenty minutes at a table, not a phone call.
Leave one thingThe listing packet or the one-page fee sheet. Something with our name on it that stays in the house.
What you bring back
The compsSold, active and expired. Expireds are the most persuasive page in the packet — they are what overpricing looks like.
Three pricesTop spot, sweet spot, sell spot, each tied to what they told you about timing and net.
Who DRG isThe database and the builder access first, ninety seconds, then a question. Don't read the deck to them.
The planCommission, marketing, and a launch calendar with real dates on it.
Something newA buyer from our database who fits their house, a release coming in a nearby community, a comp that just closed.
The second meeting is where the difference shows. Anyone can talk in a kitchen. Very few agents come back on the day they promised with the work already done.

The Close
Move 05 · Ask16

Don't talk yourself out of the listing

Once you have presented, pause. The most common way agents lose a listing they had won is by filling the silence — adding a point, softening the price, apologizing for the fee. Ask, then wait.

The ask

"Does this plan make sense to you?"

If yes: complete the information sheet and put the shoot on the calendar. If they hesitate: "What part of the plan would you like to talk through?" — and then be quiet long enough for them to answer honestly.

A no today is information, not a loss. Ask what would have to be different, write it down, and put them on the follow-up cadence. Listings expire; agents who stayed in touch get the second call.
Hesitation, and what it usually means
"We need to think"Something specific is unresolved. Ask which of the three: price, fee, or timing.
"Let's talk after the holidays"Timing is real or it's a soft no. Offer to do the prep work now so they launch the week they're ready.
"We want to try it higher"Take it — with a written two-week review. You keep the listing and the market makes your case.
Silence from one spouseAsk them directly: "How are you feeling about all this?" The quiet one usually decides.
"Send us the paperwork"Offer to bring it by instead. Paperwork emailed is paperwork unsigned.
Count to five. Silence after the ask feels twice as long to you as it does to them. Let them break it.

After the Appointment
Follow-up cadence17

The appointment isn't the end of it

Most listings are not won or lost in the room. They are won in the four weeks after it, by the only agent still showing up with something useful.

Same nightA short email: thank you, what we agreed, the three prices, and the next step with a date. Their words back to them, in writing. Every appointment, no exceptions.
Day 2The thing you promised — comps, the fee sheet, a net-proceeds estimate. Deliver early; it is the cheapest proof you are reliable.
Day 5–7One useful item, not a check-in. A comp that closed on their street, a buyer inquiry, a note about what the market did this week.
Week 2A call, not an email. "Where's your head on timing?" Ask once, directly, and accept the answer.
Week 4The neighborhood letter or a market note. From here the relationship is long-term, not transactional.
Quarterly, afterWhat sold near them and what it means for their number. Two sentences. Sellers who wait a year almost always hire the agent who kept them informed.
If they listed with someone else: send one gracious note, then a calendar reminder for day 150. A quarter of those listings are still sitting there.
Log it the same day. Route, motivation, timeline, price discussed, objection, next date. A follow-up you can't remember the details of isn't worth making.
